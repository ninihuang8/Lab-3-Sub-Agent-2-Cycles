## Claim coverage and limits
Development: E1 documents a demonstrated technical approach, not sole origin or invention priority. Practical use: E5 establishes described operational functionality; E4 establishes evaluation research, not successful deployment in our scenarios. Commercialization: E3 establishes a historical commercial offering announcement, not sales, uptake, viable customer economics, or profitability. E2 supports the architectural baseline: tool access alone does not distinguish an agent; model-directed sequencing matters. Fixed-rule automation uses predefined logic; an LLM workflow may also use tools without becoming dynamically agent-directed. This distinction is an operational interpretation of E2, not proof of breakthrough.

Degree of innovation is a criteria-based analytical judgment, not a label proven by these sources. Evidence supports comparing capability and implementation against a specified baseline; it does not establish industry-wide breakthrough, displacement, or the severity in any hypothetical case. No current market share, competitor adoption, pricing, or independent standard-ticket effectiveness claim is supported. Historical product availability is not a complete October 2026 landscape assessment.

## Conditional inference discipline for the case analysis
The following are possible mechanisms to examine, not expected conclusions or observed outcomes. None is embedded as a case conclusion.
- Shared assumptions: authorized tools, standard tickets and escalation -> if routine handling is reliable, some manual steps could be substituted and service capacity enabled -> net value depends on quality, exception workload, implementation/operating costs and conventional alternatives. No labor reduction or benefit is assumed.
- Internal support cost center -> if effort falls, capacity could be redeployed or costs avoided -> payroll savings require an actual resource change; no external ticket-hour revenue baseline is supplied. Process substitution is not automatically business-model disruption.
- Primarily staff-hour billing -> if automation reduces chargeable hours for fixed demand under unchanged hourly contracts, billable volume could fall -> costs may also fall, demand may expand, staffing/contract terms may protect value, or pricing may adapt. No revenue loss, margin effect, competitor response, or severity follows automatically.
- Unspecified pricing/revenue -> value-capture mechanisms depend on missing terms -> analyze conditional alternatives and request pricing, mix, contracts, costs and adaptation capacity. Do not substitute another case's assumptions.
- Early adoption risk depends on unmeasured reliability/control and implementation costs; failure-to-respond risk depends on unverified competitor/customer behavior. Neither risk is established as dominant.
