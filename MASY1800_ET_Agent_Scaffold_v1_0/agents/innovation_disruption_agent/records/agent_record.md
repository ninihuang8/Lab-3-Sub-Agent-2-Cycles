## E. Independent Judgment and Handoff

### Is the agent credible enough for team consideration? Why?

I think this agent is useful enough to bring to the team because it considers the differences between the cases and addresses the reasoning problem found in the first test. I would still use it with human review and evidence from the actual organization, since these tests do not establish that it is ready for deployment or can predict financial results.
I think this application is best described as a proposed process innovation. There is not enough evidence yet to call it a breakthrough. AI could reduce routine work and help the company handle more tickets, but if the company charges for actual staff hours, saving time could also mean fewer hours it can bill. In the supplementary case, my judgment is conditional High exposure because, under the case assumptions, the affected work makes up 60% of revenue and the contracts do not allow AI processing to be billed as human time. Still, Moderate could make sense if the company only uses AI for part of the work or clients agree to different pricing. I would not assume that the company will lose money or go out of business, since lower costs, the rollout scope, and changes in pricing could affect the result. For the three original cases, I think it is reasonable to leave the severity undecided until we know how much of their work, costs, or revenue would actually be affected.

### Most important recommendation to the team

I would bring the v0.2 agent package and its test records to the team. I think we should keep the distinctions between invention, innovation, commercialization, and entrepreneurship, and assess innovation degree separately from business-model disruption. We should also keep the v0.2 rule that a disruption rating needs evidence about how much of the organization’s work, costs, or revenue could be affected.
What stood out to me is that saving time can have different effects depending on how the organization works. An internal IT department might use the saved time to handle more tickets, while a company that charges for staff hours could have fewer hours to bill. In the supplementary case, the revenue share and contract terms made this more serious: about 30% of baseline revenue could be exposed if the assumed substitution is fully achieved, but that does not mean an actual 30% loss.
The supplementary case would be useful to discuss with the team as a borderline example between High and Moderate. The revenue dependence and contract restrictions support High exposure, but limited rollout or successful repricing could make Moderate more reasonable. The unspecified-pricing case is also useful for showing when the agent needs more information before judging revenue effects. When we combine our agents, we should be careful not to assume that a newer technology automatically means a bigger organizational or competitive impact.

## F. Self-Reflection

### Self-reflection

The hourly-billing case stood out to me because if AI reduces the time people spend on a task, it could also reduce what the company can charge. My first reaction was that this would mean lower revenue, but contracts, demand, and changes in pricing also matter. Saving time in an internal IT department and saving time in a company that sells working hours can therefore have different consequences. I also think people still need to evaluate the agent’s conclusions. The first Low rating is a good example: having human escalation does not tell us how much of the company’s work could be replaced. For future evaluations, I would pay more attention to the assumptions behind a rating and ask what information would change it, especially when the company’s revenue model is unclear.
